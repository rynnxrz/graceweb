Project EcoFlow is a water-retaining façade system designed to mitigate the urban heat island effect, particularly in dense metropolitan areas like London’s Oxford Street. With limited green space and high carbon
emissions, these environments suffer from overheating and reduced biodiversity. The project enhances existing buildings by increasing surface area and introducing vertical moss systems, which contribute to urban
cooling and foster cohabitation with diverse species. Over time, interconnected façades aim to form a continuous vertical urban garden, inviting ecological diversity back into the city while improving environmental
performance. EcoFlow also explores architecture as a metabolic system, re-imagining buildings as living organisms. By embedding adaptive processes into the built environment, the project positions humans not
just as observers, but as active participants within an interspecies network. This creates a resilient, symbiotic model where architecture, ecology, and human activity operate as a unified system.
