Tide Rewoven reimagines Aberdeen’s waterfront in Hong Kong as a floating, adaptive market that responds to tides, light, and ecological pressures. Rising seas, tidal surges, and declining marine biodiversity
challenge both community and economy, while conventional infrastructure remains static. Inspired by Micrasterias and algae’s phototropic efficiency, roof structures and membranes made from fishnet-chitosan
composites shift in porosity and density according to environmental conditions. Through tidal platforms, crystallizing membranes, and weather-shaped structures, it proposes a circular spatial system—one that
grows with environmental rhythms and daily use, becoming a living interface between ecology, economy, and time. Machine learning simulations anticipate environmental variations, embedding adaptability
into both form and performance.

Early research into Aberdeen’s waterfront revealed both ecological and spatial challenges. The constructed breakwater slows tidal exchange, leading to stagnation, pollution, and declining marine biodiversity, while persistent southeast winds and strong solar exposure add further
stress. Spatial analysis identified three major zones: the fish market, the boat repair yard, and the waterfront parking area. Among these, the market emerged as the main focus due to its congested layout and obstructed connection between sea and city. Fishermen must navigate
around parked vehicles to unload catches, while customers are funnelled through narrow, unsafe passages. Building on these observations, the project advances with a re-imagined fish market that integrates site waste into adaptive structures, creating a dynamic layout responsive
to tidal conditions and the changing needs of both ecology and community.

DYNAMIC DESIGN // CIRCULAR LIFE CYCLE
The market is designed as a living system that changes with the tides,
where rising and falling water levels are reshaping spaces functions
throughout the day. Where section maps how tidal rhythms, boat arrivals,
and market routines generate shifting zones for unloading, trading, and
gathering. Floating bridges are adaptive, adjusting to boat routes and
pedestrian pathways as they gather or move apart in response to activity.
Materials are developed within a circular system, where chitosan and
shell-based composites are able to decompose, reform, and reintegrate,
allowing construction to follow the same cycles as its environment. In
this way, the market is not fixed but continuously reorganized by both
ecological forces and human presence.

The roof system achieves dynamic spatial complexity through the aggregation of
individual tiles responding to varying light conditions based on the machine learning
results. Each tile adjusts its curvature in real time: flattening under intense sunlight
to minimize thermal absorption, curling maximally under moderate light to increase
shading depth, and settling into a mid-state under low light to balance brightness and
shadow. These micro-adjustments scale across the entire surface, generating a living
roof scape whose form continuously shifts with the sun. As the environmental conditions
change, the complexity of the roof’s geometry intensifies or relaxes accordingly, ensuring
energy-efficient performance through a system of distributed, light-responsive modules.
