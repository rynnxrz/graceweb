MycoTerra redefines architecture by shifting its role from static shelter to an active participant in ecological cycles. Integrating mycelium fabrication with earth construction, the project embraces
circular processes of growth, decay, and renewal, moving beyond conventional linear building methods. By working with living materials, the design respects their transformation over time and explores
transitions between life and death, solid and void. Through material science, geometric design, and digital fabrication, MycoTerra responds to site-specific environmental conditions, creating micro-
environments that nurture both human occupation and fungal growth. At its core, the project highlights a symbiotic relationship between biological and built systems—where human use and mycelial
development influence one another, generating spaces that evolve, adapt, and regenerate. By embracing the biological processes and life cycles of the soil–mycelium composite, this living system
dynamically renders itself over time, in contrast to the static urban context. (Designed with Yingying Yan & Yining Loh)

4D PRINTING ASSEMBLY
We envision the assembly of our design to be done in layers, where each layer of modules
will be cured before the next layer can be stacked and biowelded. This creates a visible
gradient as the design materialises over time. As the material continues to grow, it will
be subject to decay as well. Through mycelium growth, soil is bioremediated. Decayed
modules can be composted and reused again to print new modules, allowing the design
to evolve over time. By involving human, biological, and environmental agents in the
design process, our project hopes to give the public food for thought, to rethink of the
