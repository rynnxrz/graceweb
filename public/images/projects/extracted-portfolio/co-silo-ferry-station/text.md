CO-Silo Ferry Station is located in Wynard Point, Auckland, New Zealand, surrounded by pacific coral reefs and oceanic sea creatures. The site was a reclaimed land where used to storage fuels. The design
inspiration of the ferry station was to create a bridge between human and nature. Allowing the passengers to understand the dangerous stages that sea creatures are facing now, see the parts that were hidden
under the water. By focusing on the decaying of coral reefs, the overall design was carried out through the question: “ To what extend could architecture become a living creature that evolve and became part of
the ecology.” The site has six existing silos that were used as fuel storage. In this project I want to reverse its purpose. Rather than creating CO2, the silos are used as medians to capture CO2 and re-purpose
it to usable resource.

The design logic draws inspiration from the morphology of coral structures, translating biological principles into an architectural language that merges ecology and infrastructure. The project
investigates coral growth behaviour as a generative model for form development. Through a series of simulations, the design evolves from coral-inspired spatial divisions to the creation of tubular
building envelopes and shell-like structural systems that foster both functionality and symbolic resonance. The ferry station, positioned above the ocean surface, acts as a visible manifestation
of the invisible marine ecosystem beneath. By elevating the unseen coral logic into the public realm, the architecture raises awareness of oceanic life and environmental fragility through form,
materiality, and spatial experience.
