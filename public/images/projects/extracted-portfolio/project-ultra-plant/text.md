Project Ultra-Plant is set in a post-WW3 world, where nuclear destruction has left few liveable areas. Survivors, reliant on technology, are forced to rethink their relationship with nature, realizing a symbiotic
rather than controlling role. Project Ultra-Plant proposes a modular living system treating buildings as living organisms: humans and plants collaborate to create expandable habitats that gradually restore the
environment. The system develops in four stages, reflecting environmental clarity. As living pods increase, plant genes are collected, ultimately generating a new ecosystem. Located in Auckland’s CBD for
relative safety, the design features a central concrete core for nuclear resistance, surrounded by pods connected via plant-like veins that transfer energy and resources. Expansion of these networks supports
both human habitation and environmental recovery.

FUNDING SYSTEM
Project Ultra-Plant envisions a symbiotic living system connecting residents
to the metaverse through biological and digital exchange. Each user is
assigned a sleeping pod linked to plant-like veins that circulate nutrients
and process waste. Within the metaverse, residents design their living pods
using collected plant genes and materials, all tracked through a blockchain
network. Robots assist in gathering real-world data and resources, which are
processed by AI in the core building’s plant lab. Tested and regenerated
plant genes are then cultivated atop printed living pods, completing a closed-
loop system where human activity sustains and grows the architecture itself.

The building core functions as the central system,
collecting resources from AI vehicles, rainwater, and
solar energy through the living pods. These resources
flow via plant-like veins to the core, where the AI
classifies and redistributes them. Energy is delivered
back to residents, while human waste is absorbed by
the mycelium network as nourishment. Each living pod
is 3D-printed using materials gathered by its resident;
the core’s lower level processes these resources and
plant genes to print new pod layers. Over time, the pods
organically expand, allowing residents to emerge from
their initial sleeping space.
